01

01 - Scope & scanning method

Data sources & filtering

Public posts on Threads, saved raw into two files: posts.csv (650 raw posts, 13 columns) and comments.csv (382 raw comments across 78 posts). Before analysis, a global filtering pass was applied: removing off-topic posts (hiring/recruitment, brands outside the flower category such as pharmaceuticals/tea/other startups, non-flower gifts such as scented candles, heat packs, potted plants, and posts with no useful content) and duplicate posts by the same author with the same caption. Result: 634/650 posts (97.5%) and 378/382 comments (comments belonging to the 4 excluded posts were dropped along with them) are used for Sections 02-09. No filtering by year, since spanning 3 seasons (2024/2025/2026) is intentional, given that Oct 20 repeats every year.

Scanning method

Manually scanned through a browser, not via API: 42 queries (28 hashtags + 14 keyword phrases), capturing at most the first 5 comments per post. Post dates span from March 21, 2024 to September 21, 2026 (3 Oct 20 seasons); 209/634 posts only record relative time, falling within about 7 days before the scrape (late September 2026).

Limitations

  • Threads results favor newer/trending posts; the sample skews that way and does not represent the whole market.
  • Product category and gift-recipient tags are a "guess" based on captions, and may be inaccurate.
  • 150 posts are missing like counts, 336 posts are missing comment counts; these are counted as 0 when summing engagement.
  • 79/634 posts include a price; Section 03 further excludes prices that are not valid retail prices (the price ladder & median in Section 03 are calculated before this global filtering pass; only 1/80 priced posts was filtered out, a difference of under 2%, which does not change the conclusions).

02 - What customers are asking

21.4% of posts (139) are requests for recommendations, with a median engagement of 11, far higher than the rest (4)*. Among the 378 comments (after filtering), 30.2% ask about or mention price, and 10.3% show bulk/corporate purchase intent; 18 comments (4.8%) show both, the clearest buying signal in the entire dataset.

* The "recommendation request" and "shop/individual" classifications above are still calculated on the pre-filtering data; only 8/650 off-topic posts are affected (under 1.3%), not enough to change the conclusions.

04 - Corporate customers

47 posts are tagged "corporate gifts for female employees," with a median engagement of 24, the highest among recipient groups with at least 10 posts; captions mentioning company/office reach a median of 41. 39/382 comments (10.2%) show bulk/corporate purchase intent, with clearly stated budgets: 200k/person for 300 female employees requiring a VAT invoice (P0394), 250k/person (P0388).

This is the group that states most clearly what it needs: quantity, unit price, invoice, deadline. But most of the accounts that jump into the comments to pitch under these posts are gift shops outside flowers: scarves, scented candles, heat packs, thermal flasks. Customers have clearly said what they need, but have not yet met a seller who answers exactly what they need (VAT, quantity, deadline).

05

05 - Customer buying rhythm

Across the two complete seasons (2024-2025, 240 posts with absolute dates, after filtering), 44.6% of posts fall within the final week plus Oct 20 itself. The single busiest posting day is Oct 20 itself, followed by D-3 and D-4.

Monday is the busiest posting day in the 28 days before the holiday (35/170 posts, calculated pre-filtering; Tuesday is lowest, at 15). The 2026 season is trending upward: September 1-21 alone already has 68 posts (after filtering), and most of the 209 posts that only record relative time almost certainly fall within the final week before the holiday.

07

07 - Customer insights

Evidence strength: multiple independent sources point the same way · a single source, not yet cross-checked.

Customers ask about price in public because they fear being overcharged for the holiday, not out of curiosity

30.2% of comments ask about or mention price, but only 12.5% of posts publish a price; the two highest-engagement posts in the entire dataset (8,587 and 8,573) are both arguments over "unusually cheap" and "delusional pricing," and real sellers repeatedly have to explain that wholesale prices rise 2-3x for the holiday. Customers ask upfront because they lack information, not because they enjoy engaging.

What might be wrong: the two viral posts may have been boosted by the algorithm for being controversial, not necessarily because of the pricing topic alone.

"What should I give" matters more to customers than a ready-made ad post

Keywords customers type themselves ("quà 20/10", "20/10 tặng gì"…) have a median engagement of 31.5, almost 16 times that of hashtags (2); question/recommendation-request posts are only 21.4% of posts but account for 61.6% of total engagement. Customers look for validation from the community before deciding, rather than scrolling past ready-made shop content.

What might be wrong: the "question/recommendation-request" classification is based on question marks and a few fixed phrases, and may miss other ways of asking.

Corporate customers already have a clear budget and deadline, but have not yet found the right seller

The "corporate gifts for female employees" tag has the highest median engagement (24) among recipient groups large enough to compare; 10.3% of comments show bulk purchase intent; budgets are stated clearly at 200-250k/person with a VAT invoice requirement, but most of the people responding are non-flower gift shops.

What might be wrong: 70.8% of posts have no recipient tag; there may be another, even higher-engagement customer group hidden in the untagged portion.

Customers buying a month before the holiday think differently from those buying during the holiday week itself

44.6% of posts cluster into the final week plus the holiday itself (2024-2025 seasons combined); but the three largest corporate-gift inquiry posts of the 2026 season already appeared September 9-16, nearly a month before Oct 20. Corporate customers make decisions much earlier than the crowd.

What might be wrong: only 3 anchor posts; the sample is too small to treat as a recurring yearly pattern.

The biggest worry is not price, but "the ad photo doesn't match what customers actually get"

A recurring worry in real comments is that the flowers received don't match the advertising photo ("It's obvious the shop used way fewer flowers than in the photo" - P0588); meanwhile only 9/634 captions proactively mention real photos, feedback, or reviews to reassure customers before they ask.

What might be wrong: a qualitative observation drawn from a few specific comments; the share of customers who actually worry about this across all 378 comments has not been counted.

11 - Gaps

A public price list that explains holiday pricing

Only 12.3% of posts include a price; the 9 posts complaining about price have a median engagement of 26. No shop has yet turned a price explanation into core content.

Corporate packages, with VAT, priced per person

Customers give a range of 100-250k/person and need an invoice; the comments under these posts are being taken over by non-flower shops.

Proof that "the sample photo equals what you receive"

Only 9 captions mention real photos/feedback, while 2 "photo doesn't match reality" posts sparked long arguments.

Durable, practical gifts for moms

Captions mentioning "mom" have a median engagement of 33 but only 34 posts; the "durable/never wilts" group has just 12 captions.

Care instructions for after receiving flowers

Only 3 captions give care instructions, even though comments mention a third of the flowers spoiling after one day.

12 - Next steps

  1. This week: lock in corporate customers

    Package 3 tiers at 100k/150k/250k per person, clearly noting VAT is included. Respond to posts asking about corporate gifts within 24 hours with a concrete quote.

  2. Publish a 3-tier price ladder with "holiday pricing"

    One pinned post: under 100k, 100-299k, 300-699k, each tier with a real photo and a reason why holiday pricing differs from regular days.

  3. Write the way buyers search

    Use the phrases they type themselves, "quà 20/10," "hoa tặng mẹ 20/10," opening posts with a question or a price figure instead of a string of hashtags.

  4. Build a "gift for mom" product line

    Durable flowers (wax, crochet, potted plants) or small fresh bouquets paired with everyday items.

  5. Concentrate effort from D-7 to Oct 20

    Increase posting frequency, include real photos of the bouquet before delivery, and a care instruction card.

  6. After the holiday: re-scan and compare

    Repeat the exact same 42 queries during the week of October 21-31, adding hashtags that returned 0 posts and the original targets.

Good for

  • Choosing a content direction and price range to test this season, based on the sample sizes stated.
  • Targeting one specific gap to test first (corporate packages, the "gift for mom" line).
  • Serving as a baseline for the next scan, to compare before/after.

Not yet good for

  • Claiming the real market size; the data is only posts that surfaced in 42 queries.
  • Comparing market share; the 5 accounts in Section 10 are illustrative, not the full set of competitors.
  • Finalizing a selling price; the price ladder in Section 03 is based on 51/650 posts.

Engagement = likes + comments (blank cells count as 0, "8.4K" is converted to 8400); relative dates are assigned to the final week before the scrape. The "shop account / individual user" classification in Section 02 and the price groups in Section 03 are manual coding and cannot be reproduced 100% when refiltered. The results are directional signals, not representative of the whole market.
